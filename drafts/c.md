OPEN INTEREST
The daily brief, format v2. Edition of 15 November 2026.

LEAD

The Justice Department moves to seize $1.2bn stolen from Binance and takes down the servers behind North Korea's fake video calls.

The Department of Justice announced on 12 November 2026 that a court had authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2bn stolen from Binance. The same announcement disclosed the takedown of FaceStealer, a webcam-recording scheme the FBI links to more than $400m in thefts over 18 months.

The Department's 12 November 2026 release says members of the North Korean military, which the FBI calls "TraderTraitor", stole more than $6bn in virtual assets from Binance on or about 29 October 2026 [https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency]. BleepingComputer puts the theft at about $6.4bn [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-disrupts-north-korean-campaign-behind-400-million-crypto-theft/]. Government and private-sector freezes total more than $2.7bn, and the court-authorized $1.2bn is part of that sum [https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency]. Against the Department's own floor of $6bn stolen, that is about a fifth secured in court and under half frozen. Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva said the Criminal Division helped "halt about half of this historic breach" [https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency]. The release says laundering continues through bridges, mixers and exchanges [https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency].

FaceStealer worked through trust. Invitations arrived from accounts stolen from earlier victims. Inside the call, the attackers played recorded webcam video of people the target knew, then staged a fake audio fault whose "fix" installed malware [https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency]. The FBI seized over 900 webcam videos of victims under a federal warrant [https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency]. The Bureau attributes the activity to the group tracked as Sapphire Sleet, UNC1069 and BlueNoroff [https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/facestealer-cryptocurrency-theft-scheme].

The target list is widening. The FBI says the actors increasingly pursue open-source maintainers, aiming at the software supply chain [https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/facestealer-cryptocurrency-theft-scheme]. BleepingComputer, citing the FBI, reports one attempt compromised the maintainer of a JavaScript library with tens of millions of weekly downloads before detection stopped it [https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-disrupts-north-korean-campaign-behind-400-million-crypto-theft/]. The release names no recovered amount returned to Binance customers.

NEWS

[NEWS] Unocoin says eight-year-old paper backup keys signed its unauthorized transfers.
The Indian exchange said on 14 November 2026 that encrypted backup keys, printed on paper about eight years ago and kept in physical storage, were compromised and used to authorize transactions from "a small number" of hot wallets [https://blog.unocoin.com/security-incident-update/]. Unocoin first detected unauthorized activity on 6 November 2026 and asked Fireblocks and BitGo to freeze its accounts at 11:24 and 11:29 UTC that day [https://blog.unocoin.com/security-incident-update/]. BitGo said its systems were not breached and the transactions were signed with the user and backup keys Unocoin held, as reported by crypto.news [https://crypto.news/bitgo-unocoin-transfers-client-held-keys/]. Unocoin has published no loss figure and says it still cannot explain how the keys were compromised [https://blog.unocoin.com/security-incident-update/].

[NEWS] The White House nominates two SEC and two CFTC commissioners, including Hester Peirce's successor.
Nominations sent to the Senate on 12 November 2026 name Kristin N. Johnson and Steven Levine to the SEC, and Nathan Anonick and Matthew Rowland to the CFTC [https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/nominations-sent-to-the-senate-16a6/]. Levine would take the seat of Hester Peirce, whose term expired, for a term running to 5 June 2030 [https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/nominations-sent-to-the-senate-16a6/]. The Block reports that Anonick and Rowland both worked on the Clarity Act, and that these are the first such nominations in roughly a year [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-12-white-house-crypto-picks-sec-cftc-439175]. Johnson is a former CFTC commissioner, per The Block [https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-12-white-house-crypto-picks-sec-cftc-439175].

[NEWS] House Oversight staff propose limits on prediction-market trading by federal personnel.
The House Oversight Committee released a staff memorandum on 13 November 2026 on suspected insider trading by officials, military personnel and clearance holders on prediction markets [https://oversight.house.gov/release/house-oversight-committee-releases-memorandum-on-insider-trading-risks-in-prediction-markets/]. It recommends restrictions on federal personnel in all branches, from an outright ban to limits on government-related contracts [https://oversight.house.gov/release/house-oversight-committee-releases-memorandum-on-insider-trading-risks-in-prediction-markets/]. The memo says existing insider-trading law under the Commodity Exchange Act already applies, and credits the platforms with flagging most reported incidents first [https://oversight.house.gov/release/house-oversight-committee-releases-memorandum-on-insider-trading-risks-in-prediction-markets/]. Decrypt reports the inquiry drew on nearly 1,700 pages of documents and five staff briefings [https://decrypt.co/383271/government-officials-banned-prediction-markets-house-oversight].

TECH

[TECH] A fee-market paper finds Ethereum's state budget runs about a quarter below target.
Fei Wu, writing from an Ethereum Foundation internship, posted "More Meters or More Prices?" on 12 November 2026, comparing one- and multi-dimensional fee designs [https://ethresear.ch/t/26244]. Under the strongest one-dimensional benchmark, the post reports 151.9M to 177.9M execution gas per block but state growth of 86.0 to 88.1 GiB a year, against a 120 GiB target [https://ethresear.ch/t/26244]. The author finds the EIP-1559 update rule cannot push the base fee to the few-wei level a high target needs, and tests the EIP-4844 style update instead [https://ethresear.ch/t/26244]. The results rest on a demand model calibrated from the February and July 2025 gas-limit increases, which the post states plainly [https://ethresear.ch/t/26244].

[TECH] A researcher argues Ethereum should size its capacity by measured strain.
Csaba Kiraly's 11 November 2026 opinion post, "Elastic Ethereum", says the chain fixes capacity in advance and has no protocol-wide rule adapting limits to resource pressure [https://ethresear.ch/t/26236]. He cites mainnet data: the median block reaches 95% of reporting nodes 0.38 seconds after first sighting, against an attestation deadline 4 seconds into the slot [https://ethresear.ch/t/26236]. Most of that margin sits idle, he argues, while late blocks still miss the deadline at some nodes [https://ethresear.ch/t/26236]. The post names TCP congestion control as its model and describes itself as a direction, not a concrete design [https://ethresear.ch/t/26236].

CULTURE

[CULTURE] Molly White says crypto's midterm money mostly backed losers in contested races.
White's 13 November 2026 analysis says crypto and AI super PACs spent $250m on the midterms, with crypto's Fairshake going 74 for 74 where its candidate was favored but 10 for 18 elsewhere [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/]. She counts $64m spent to keep Sherrod Brown out of the Senate; he won anyway [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/]. Of 57 crypto super PAC ads she tracked, she found three that mentioned crypto [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/]. Fairshake told Bloomberg it "played a meaningful role in winning competitive races", a claim White disputes with her dollar-weighted tally [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/].

[CULTURE] DeFi Education Fund leads an industry brief against Illinois' digital asset tax.
The DeFi Education Fund said on 12 November 2026 that it led a coalition, with Andreessen Horowitz and Uniswap among the co-leads, filing an amicus brief in Sangamon County court [https://www.defieducationfund.org/def-amici-submit-amicus-brief-illinois-digital-asset-tax-act-is-unlawful-and-unenforceable/]. The brief argues the Digital Asset Tax Act is vague, overbroad and discriminatory toward blockchain transactions [https://www.defieducationfund.org/def-amici-submit-amicus-brief-illinois-digital-asset-tax-act-is-unlawful-and-unenforceable/]. It cites an estimate that 2.64 million Illinois residents hold digital assets, the fifth-highest total of any state [https://www.defieducationfund.org/def-amici-submit-amicus-brief-illinois-digital-asset-tax-act-is-unlawful-and-unenforceable/]. The argument is the coalition's; the court has not ruled on it.
