OPEN INTEREST: EDITION OF 15 NOVEMBER 2026 (format v2)

LEAD

The Justice Department moves to seize $1.2 billion of the Binance haul.

A federal court has authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion stolen from Binance. The Justice Department says North Korean military hackers took the funds, and that U.S. and private-sector freezes now cover more than $2.7 billion.

The Justice Department announced the action on 12 November 2026 in release 26-1356 [https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency]. It says members of North Korea's military, which the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about 29 October 2026. The release does not name the court. The Block, which reported the announcement, also found no case number or order date in the public record [https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-11-12-doj-seize-1-2-billion-binance-hack-north-korea-facestealer-439065].

The figures do not match across sources. Binance put its own loss at about $6.3 billion and set aside $6.34 billion to cover it, according to The Block. The department says "more than $6 billion." Seizure authority over $1.2 billion is therefore roughly 19 percent of Binance's own loss figure and under half of the $2.7 billion already frozen. Binance reported about $3 billion frozen, while Chainalysis estimated about $2.8 billion and TRM Labs about $2.7 billion [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Binance_hack].

Attribution is now an official U.S. government position. The FBI named North Korea on 31 October 2026 [https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2026/north-korea-responsible-for-6-billion-binance-attack]. On 3 November 2026 North Korea's Kim Yo Jong denied involvement, in a statement carried by state media [http://kcna.kp/en/article/detail/873496f71f6ab3e667a4271b5a4cf4c6]. Journalist Molly White wrote on 29 October 2026 that the unrecovered remainder exceeds Binance's insurance fund [https://www.citationneeded.news/binance-theft/].

This is a new development after the 2 November 2026 edition test that led on Binance withdrawals. A seizure order is a legal step, not a recovery. The release gives no wallet addresses, no asset breakdown and no timeline for returning funds. Whether the Bitcoin share, about $1.5 billion that could not be frozen per the Wikipedia timeline, falls inside the $1.2 billion is unanswered. Investigators say they are still tracing assets moved through bridges, mixers and exchanges [https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-11-12-doj-seize-1-2-billion-binance-hack-north-korea-facestealer-439065].

NEWS

[NEWS] The FBI seizes 900 victim webcam videos in the FaceStealer case.
The Justice Department seized cloud infrastructure behind a North Korean campaign the FBI calls FaceStealer, announced on 12 November 2026. The FBI says the campaign stole more than $400 million in crypto over 18 months. Attackers used accounts taken from earlier victims to send video-call invitations, played recordings or deepfakes of people the target knew, then faked an audio fault to push a malware "fix". Targets included crypto workers and open-source developers, and the FBI published a victim hub at fbi.gov/facestealer [https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency].

[NEWS] An attacker drains Kava Lend of about 55.2 million KAVA.
The exploit hit the lending market on 14 November 2026 through a dormant BUSD market, according to reports. CryptoNews reported that Upbit, Bithumb and Coinone suspended KAVA deposits and withdrawals [https://cryptonews.net/news/security/33752914/]. Kava said the incident was isolated to Kava Lend and was not an infinite mint, per KuCoin News [https://www.kucoin.com/news/insight/KAVA/6af89da440903b0007ae0adc]. Reported losses range from about $3.3 million to $3.8 million, and no outlet had a Kava statement on repaying lenders [https://coincentral.com/a-missing-zero-check-cost-kava-lend-3-8-million-in-one-night].

[NEWS] BitGo says Unocoin's own keys signed the disputed transfers.
In a 13 November 2026 statement on X, BitGo said it was not breached, as reported by The Defiant [https://thedefiant.io/news/security/bitgo-denies-breach-as-unocoin-probes-wallet-transfers]. BitGo said the 2-of-3 wallet's transactions used Unocoin's user and backup keys, and that its own key was never used. Unocoin has suspended services since 9 November 2026 and has given no loss figure, according to crypto.news [https://crypto.news/bitgo-unocoin-transfers-client-held-keys/]. How the backup key was obtained is unresolved.

TECH

[TECH] Ethereum researchers propose a protocol that scales its workload down under load.
Csaba Kiraly posted "Elastic Ethereum" on 11 November 2026. He argues that Ethereum's capacity is fixed in advance and should instead react to measured pressure, using the size of the unfinalized chain as the control signal. He cites Xatu data from 100,548 blocks: the median block reaches 95 percent of nodes in 0.38 seconds, and late head events past the 4-second attestation deadline hit 1.7 percent of blocks in one node group and 11.0 percent in the other [https://ethresear.ch/t/elastic-ethereum/26236]. It is an opinion piece, not a specification.

[TECH] PropAMMs carry an estimated 5 to 10 percent of Ethereum swap volume.
Mike Neuder and Maryam Bahrani wrote on 7 October 2026 that market-maker-run AMMs raise the barrier to block building, because makers must integrate directly with builders. In a 12 November 2026 follow-up, DanGuo reported that PropAMM-fill transactions paid builders about $61.4 thousand over 30 days, against about $20.0 thousand in spread paid to makers. Builders without maker deals made 14 percent of blocks but only 4 of roughly 87,000 relevant fills [https://ethresear.ch/t/ethereum-propamms-today-and-tomorrow/26129].

CULTURE

[CULTURE] Crypto's election spending mostly backed losers in close races.
Molly White reported on 13 November 2026 that crypto and AI super PACs spent about $250 million on the midterms. Fairshake spent $154.5 million and went 74 for 74 where its candidate was favored, but 10 for 18 in competitive races. Crypto PACs spent $64 million against Sherrod Brown in Ohio, and he won. Fairshake says it "played a meaningful role"; White argues win rates count safe seats, not dollars [https://www.citationneeded.news/crypto-ai-lost-the-midterms/].

[CULTURE] Arthur Hayes argues AI share dilution will hurt crypto.
In a 5 November 2026 essay, Hayes estimates that Anthropic's shares would be diluted 83 percent by June 2027 based on its S-1, and that a $1.5 trillion market cap would need about $1.245 trillion of new capital to hold flat. He says falling AI equities would tighten bank balance sheets before a central bank rescue. These are his estimates, some made with an AI agent reading filings, and he discloses he advises projects he promotes [https://cryptohayes.substack.com/p/d-is-for-dilution]. The essay is commentary, not a forecast Open Interest endorses.
